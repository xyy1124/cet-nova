Good morning, everyone. Today I want to talk about something we all do but rarely think about: sleep.
For a long time, sleep was regarded as a passive state, a kind of shutdown after a busy day.
Recent research, however, suggests that the brain remains remarkably active during sleep.
In one experiment, students learned a list of word pairs in the afternoon.
Half of them slept for ninety minutes, while the other half stayed awake.
The group that slept recalled about twenty percent more pairs the next morning.
What seems to matter is not simply the total amount of sleep, but the deep stages of it.
So if you are preparing for an exam, a short nap may be more useful than another hour of cramming.
